About

The short version of a longer story.

I spent my early twenties in service bays, not spreadsheets — running OBD-II scans, tracing electrical faults with a multimeter, and translating "check engine" into a plain-English explanation a customer could act on. That job taught me something a classroom can't: how to isolate one true cause inside a system full of noisy, conflicting signals.

While I worked, I was also finishing a B.S. in Economics with a Mathematics minor at the University of Houston, building the other half of the toolkit — regression analysis, statistics, and machine learning — that turns a hunch into a defensible answer. Data analysis is the place those two halves meet: pull the numbers apart, find the pattern underneath, and explain it so clearly that the next decision makes itself.

I'm most energized by problems with a customer on the other end of the answer — forecasting, churn, operations — anywhere a dataset is standing in for a real person trying to make a real decision.
